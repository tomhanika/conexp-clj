#+title: DimFlux

* DimFlux

~DimFlux~ draws the concept lattice of a formal context as a /doubly-additive/
line diagram.  In such a diagram every concept node sits at the sum of a set of
vectors, one for each irreducible object below it and one for each irreducible
attribute above it, which is what makes additive diagrams full of parallelograms
and, as a consequence, comparatively easy to read.

The algorithm runs in three steps:

1. an initial diagram is computed with [[https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.00686][DimDraw]], which extends the order relation
   until it has order dimension two and reads the coordinates off the resulting
   realizer;
2. that diagram is orthogonally projected onto the space of doubly-additive
   diagrams, so that it becomes additive while staying as close to the DimDraw
   diagram as possible;
3. the element vectors are refined by a force-directed placement which maximizes
   the /conflict distance/, the distance between a concept node and the edges it
   is not incident with.

It is described in M. Nöhre, D. Dürrschnabel, B. Ganter and G. Stumme, /DimFlux:
Force-Directed Additive Line Diagrams/, International Journal of Approximate
Reasoning 197 (2026) 109734, [[https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16366]].

** Drawing a lattice

~dim-flux-layout~ takes a formal context or a lattice and returns a layout:

#+begin_src clojure :results silent
(use 'conexp.fca.contexts)
(use 'conexp.fca.lattices)
(use 'conexp.layouts.dim-flux)

(def ctx (read-context "testing-data/living_beings_and_water.cxt"))

(draw-layout (dim-flux-layout ctx))
#+end_src

Passing the layout function to ~draw-lattice~ works as well:

#+begin_src clojure :results silent
(draw-lattice (concept-lattice ctx)
              :layout-fn dim-flux-layout)
#+end_src

In the lattice editor, DimFlux is available under the layout choice next to
DimDraw, Freese and Force.  The ~Iterations~ spinner bounds the number of
conjugate gradient steps of the refinement, and ~Additive~ projects the diagram
currently on screen onto the closest doubly-additive one without running the
refinement.

** Parameters

A map given directly after the lattice overrides the defaults in
~default-parameters~; every further argument is handed on to DimDraw:

#+begin_src clojure :results silent
;; more weight on the repulsion between nodes and non-incident edges
(dim-flux-layout ctx {:w-rep 100.0})

;; no refinement at all, i.e. the projected DimDraw diagram
(dim-flux-layout ctx {:iterations 0})

;; let DimDraw use its greedy instead of its exact two-dimension extension
(dim-flux-layout ctx {} "greedy")
#+end_src

| Parameter       | Meaning                                                          | Default |
|-----------------+------------------------------------------------------------------+---------|
| ~:w-rep~        | weight of the repulsion between nodes and non-incident edges      |    50.0 |
| ~:w-att~        | weight of the attraction along the edges                          |     1.0 |
| ~:w-grav~       | weight of the gravitation keeping the element vectors oriented    |    30.0 |
| ~:iterations~   | bound on the number of conjugate gradient iterations              |    1000 |
| ~:min-distance~ | smallest conflict distance the repulsive energy is evaluated at   |   0.001 |

The refinement is a local optimization and may fail to produce a valid line
diagram.  In that case ~dim-flux-layout~ falls back to the projected diagram,
and if that one is invalid as well, to the DimDraw diagram, so that the result is
always a line diagram of the lattice.

** Working with additive diagrams

Two functions of the namespace are useful on their own.  ~additive-layout~
returns the doubly-additive layout closest to a given one, the orthogonal
projection onto the space of additive placements:

#+begin_src clojure :results silent
(additive-layout (standard-layout (concept-lattice ctx)))
#+end_src

and ~doubly-additive?~ decides whether a layout already is additive, which is
exactly the case when it equals its own projection:

#+begin_src clojure :results silent
(doubly-additive? (dim-flux-layout ctx))       ;; => true
(doubly-additive? (standard-layout (concept-lattice ctx)))
#+end_src

Because the projection is cheap, it can also be applied after every step of an
interactive edit: dragging a node destroys additivity, and projecting restores
it, at the price of moving the other nodes along.

** Limitations

Not every concept lattice has a diagram that is both realizer-embedded and
additive; the free modular lattice on three generators is a counterexample given
in the paper.  The projection therefore genuinely changes the DimDraw diagram in
general, and the refinement optimizes from there.

Like DimDraw, the algorithm is meant for the lattice sizes that are actually
drawn by hand, roughly up to 20 to 30 concepts.  Its cost is dominated by the
two-dimension extension of DimDraw; the projection and the force-directed
refinement are comparatively cheap.
